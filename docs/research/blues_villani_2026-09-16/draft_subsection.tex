\paragraph{Revisiting the Blues optimization examples.}
We reconstructed the first-order chord-generation experiments of
\cite{DBLP:journals/constraints/PachetR11} using Snarky, a Python symbolic
inference and finite-domain constraint engine. The training set comprises 22
C-normalized Blues choruses selected from LSDB, represented by 24 half-bar symbols
per chorus and augmented through all 12 transpositions. Tune/take multiplicities
and sequence boundaries are retained. For Boulez Blues, both training and
generation use the 24 dominant-seventh and minor chords, under an explicitly
documented reduction of chord qualities before frequency estimation.
Every generated sequence starts on C7, has F7 at position 9, and ends on G7 at
position 24. The exotic example additionally requires exactly one F-sharp seventh;
Boulez Blues instead requires all 24 chords to be different.

The objective is the exact rational product of the initial symbol marginal and
23 empirical first-order transition probabilities. Ordinary and exotic optima
are independently verified by dynamic programming, with an occurrence counter in
the latter case. For Boulez Blues, completed branch-and-bound regenerates the
exact chord sequence printed in Table~5 of the 2011 paper and certifies its
optimality under the reconstructed model. Its natural-log score is
$-46.921592$, compared with the historical reported value $-46.74$.
This difference reflects distinct corpus or preprocessing conventions; the full
historical preprocessing has not been recovered, and identity of the trained
probability models is not claimed.

An exact maximum-product assignment relaxation provides an admissible bound for
the all-different path problem. In three unseeded measurements, native preparation
and search complete in a median of $1.732$ seconds and 209 nodes; the optimal
incumbent is reached at a median controller time of $0.272$ seconds. A chain-bound
reference and a controller-only ablation both time out at five seconds without
reaching that score. These measurements use Python 3.13.11 on an arm64 macOS host;
training and model construction are excluded. They demonstrate an improvement in
solution quality and proof completion within a fixed budget, not a measured
speedup ratio against the historical Backtalk implementation.
